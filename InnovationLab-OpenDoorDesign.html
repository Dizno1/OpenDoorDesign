Innovation Lab

Ideas Become Prototypes. Prototypes Become Solutions.

The Innovation Lab is the workshop side of Open Door Design. It is where accessibility challenges become experiments, experiments become prototypes, and prototypes become tools that can increase independence.

The Lab is not a product catalog. It is a living space for research, testing, learning, rebuilding, and trying again.

Some ideas are early concepts. Some are active development efforts. Some may become public tools. Others may simply teach us a better way to design.

Available During Development

This site and its projects remain under active development. All current content is available for people to access, test, use, and critique while the work continues.

What the Lab Is For

The Innovation Lab exists because many accessibility barriers are not solved by adding labels to existing visual designs.

Sometimes the entire workflow needs to be reimagined from a screen reader-first perspective.

The Lab explores accessible document workflows, media creation tools, study systems, practice environments, audio-based training, and other ideas that can help people work, learn, create, and participate with greater independence.

Screen Reader First Innovation

Every Lab project begins with the same question.

How would this work if a person could not see the screen?

That question changes the design process. It pushes the work toward clearer structure, better keyboard support, meaningful status feedback, efficient navigation, and workflows that do not depend on sighted interpretation.

When a tool works well for screen reader and keyboard users, it often becomes simpler and more usable for everyone.

Active Lab Projects

Document Remediation Tool

A successor to the earlier document tagging proof of concept, focused on helping screen reader users review, structure, and improve accessible documents with greater independence.

Status: Active development

Open Door Accessible Assistant

The assistant began as a media-focused workflow application and is evolving into a broader accessibility-first system that accepts plain-language goals and handles provider and workflow complexity behind the scenes.

Its current features represent only an early layer of its potential. New media, document, web, EPUB, and accessibility-support capabilities will be exposed as they become ready for meaningful use and critique.

Status: Active development

Open the Open Door Accessible Assistant

AccessibleScreenCapture

A traditionally visual screenshot and screen-recording workflow is being rebuilt around nonvisual confirmation, queued review, meaningful status, keyboard operation, and accessible editing. The free 2.1.0 edition and Pro 3.0.0 Beta 14 demonstrate how Screen Reader First design can change the workflow itself rather than merely label it.

Status: 2.1.0 planned free edition; Pro 3.0.0 Beta 14 in active testing

UniversalMediaController

Research into whether one accessibility-first controller can provide consistent operation across supported existing media players. The HTML5 foundation is working, with experimental Vimeo integration extending the interoperability research.

Status: Active development

Accessible Media Creation

Accessible Video Suite, Audio Description Author, and Accessible Transcript Editor continue as active projects exploring nonvisual media review, editing, description, transcript, segment, preview, and export workflows. They also inform broader ODD research without being absorbed into a single application.

Status: Active development

Why Prototypes Matter

Prototypes are not failures because they are unfinished.

They are how better questions are discovered.

A prototype can reveal an interaction problem, expose a missing status message, prove that a workflow is too visual, or show that a simpler approach is possible.

In the Innovation Lab, every prototype is a teacher.

Connected to the Academy

The Innovation Lab and the Accessibility Academy are separate spaces, but they are built to strengthen each other.

The Academy teaches accessibility concepts. The Lab tests those concepts through real tools and experiments.

Every Lab project can become a lesson. Every Academy lesson can inspire a better Lab project.

Explore the Accessibility Academy

Help Shape the Lab

The strongest accessibility ideas come from people who encounter barriers, test workflows, ask better questions, and are willing to challenge assumptions.

The Innovation Lab welcomes feedback from screen reader users, keyboard users, developers, designers, educators, researchers, athletes, students, accessibility professionals, and organizations.

If you have a barrier to solve, an idea to explore, or a workflow that needs to become more accessible, the Lab is where that conversation can begin.
