Open Door Design

Opening Doors Through Accessibility, Education, Innovation, and Community

Open Door Design is a growing accessibility initiative dedicated to improving independence through screen reader-first design, practical accessibility education, inclusive technology, and community collaboration.

This is more than a collection of accessibility projects. It is the beginning of a community where lived experience, technical knowledge, creativity, and shared purpose come together to remove barriers and create opportunity.

Open Door Design Is In Its Founding Season

You are not visiting a finished organization. You are witnessing the beginning of one.

The Academy is still growing. The Innovation Lab is still experimenting. Some ideas will become tools. Some prototypes will teach us a better way. Every conversation, every accessibility review, every shared experience, and every collaborator will help shape what Open Door Design becomes.

The shell has cracked. Help us discover what emerges.

Screen Reader First

This website was designed from a Screen Reader First perspective.

Most websites are designed visually and adapted for accessibility later. Open Door Design begins from the opposite direction. Every page, heading structure, landmark region, navigation pattern, link, and keyboard interaction begins with the experience of someone navigating without sight.

That approach does not create a separate experience. It creates a better one. Clear structure, predictable navigation, meaningful links, and keyboard-first interaction make the site easier to understand, faster to explore, and more comfortable for everyone.

We invite visitors to notice the difference. Accessibility by design is not a constraint. It is a blueprint for better usability.

Why Open Door Design Exists

Open Door Design exists because someone opened a door.

The Carroll Center for the Blind demonstrated that blindness does not limit independence. The Blind Institute of Technology expanded that foundation through education, mentorship, professional development, accessibility training, and opportunity.

Those opportunities changed more than a career path. They created a mission.

Open Door Design exists to continue that tradition by building tools, learning systems, research projects, and community pathways that help others gain independence, confidence, and access.

The goal is not simply to create software. The goal is to create opportunity.

Every Project Has a Story

The SR Document Remediation Tool did not begin as a product. It began with a simple desire to remediate documents independently without unnecessary sighted assistance.

Before it became the current direction, there was a proof of concept. That early document tagging experiment taught valuable lessons and led to a stronger successor focused on practical document remediation.

Many Open Door Design initiatives follow that same path. A barrier appears. A question follows. An experiment begins. A prototype teaches. A better solution emerges.

That process is not accidental. It is how accessibility innovation grows.

Current Initiatives

Current initiatives are not presented as finished products. They are active explorations, prototypes, learning systems, and accessibility concepts shaped by practical need and community feedback.

SR Document Remediation Tool

A screen reader-first document remediation concept focused on helping blind users review, structure, and improve accessible documents with greater independence.

Active development

Audio Description Tool

A workflow concept for creating and reviewing audio description, timing, narration support, and non-visual media access.

Prototype concept

Accessible Video Suite

A screen reader-first media editing concept focused on keyboard control, efficient non-visual workflows, and accessible media interaction.

Active exploration

CPACC Accessibility Academy

A developing study path for CPACC preparation, accessibility vocabulary, disability concepts, standards, laws, usability principles, and professional practice.

Founding stage

WCAG Accessibility Lab

A practical learning environment for WCAG examples, screen reader testing, defect writing, issue recognition, and accessibility decision-making.

Founding stage

Beepball Practice Caller App

An audio training concept designed to support beep baseball practice through randomized calls, stereo audio cues, and screen reader accessible interaction.

Concept development

Accessible Carousel Initiative

A public demonstration showing how carousel patterns can support images, video, forms, links, and interactive content without excluding screen reader, keyboard, or mobile assistive technology users.
