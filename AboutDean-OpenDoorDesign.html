About Dean

Accessibility Engineer, Tester, Educator, and Screen Reader User

My work focuses on practical accessibility, screen reader usability, inclusive design, and tools that help people understand and remove barriers in digital experiences.

I use JAWS as my primary screen reader, and that lived experience shapes how I evaluate design, usability, training, documentation, media, forms, and interaction patterns.

How I Work

I approach accessibility as a combination of technical structure, human usability, assistive technology support, and practical communication.

  • I test digital experiences with screen reader and keyboard workflows.
  • I look for barriers that affect real task completion, not only automated tool results.
  • I explain accessibility issues in plain language that developers, designers, clients, and non-technical users can understand.
  • I focus on practical fixes that improve independence and reduce friction.

Focus Areas

Screen Reader Usability

Testing and improving workflows for users who rely on screen readers, keyboard navigation, meaningful labels, headings, live regions, and clear interaction patterns.

Accessibility Education

Developing learning tools, practice systems, challenge workflows, and plain-language explanations that help people understand accessibility concepts.

Accessible Media

Exploring audio description, accessible video editing, narration workflows, and multimedia access for users who cannot rely on visual information alone.

Document Accessibility

Improving workflows involving tagged PDFs, headings, forms, alternate text, reading order, and assistive technology review.

Tools and Assistive Technology

My testing and development work often includes JAWS, keyboard-only navigation, browser-based testing, accessible HTML patterns, WCAG 2.2 AA concepts, document remediation workflows, and accessibility-focused prototypes.

What Clients and Collaborators Can Expect

Clients and collaborators can expect direct communication, practical recommendations, accessible documentation, usability-focused feedback, and a strong focus on real-world task completion for assistive technology users.
