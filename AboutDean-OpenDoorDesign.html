About Open Door Design

Opening Doors Through Accessibility

Open Door Design was created from a simple belief: accessibility should not be treated as an afterthought, a compliance checkbox, or a final step added after a product has already been built.

Accessibility should be part of the design process from the beginning. Open Door Design exists to help create technology, learning systems, media experiences, and digital tools that support independence, usability, and equal access for everyone.

The long-term vision is to build a collaborative accessibility organization where people with disabilities, accessibility professionals, educators, developers, designers, apprentices, and community partners can work together to solve real-world accessibility challenges.

Whether the work involves document accessibility, screen reader usability, accessible media, audio description, learning systems, accessibility education, or inclusive technology design, the goal remains the same.

  • Remove barriers.
  • Increase independence.
  • Create opportunity.

How Open Door Design Began

The story of Open Door Design did not begin in technology.

For more than thirty years, Dean built a successful career as a Randolph-Sheppard vendor. That career provided independence, purpose, and the opportunity to serve others. Like many businesses across the country, everything changed during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The location Dean operated on an Air Force Base was unable to recover from the impact of the shutdowns, creating the need to explore an entirely new direction.

What initially appeared to be the end of one career became the beginning of another.

Searching for new opportunities led Dean to the Screen Reader User Tester Training program at the Carroll Center for the Blind. That experience introduced the world of accessibility, usability, assistive technology, and digital inclusion.

More importantly, it revealed something unexpected.

Accessibility was not simply a profession. It was an opportunity to help create meaningful change.

The Organizations That Opened Doors

Open Door Design would not exist without the organizations and people who helped create opportunities along the way.

The Carroll Center for the Blind provided the foundation through Screen Reader User Tester Training. The program introduced accessibility testing, assistive technology evaluation, and the broader accessibility community.

That experience eventually led to the Blind Institute of Technology.

The impact of BIT on the creation of Open Door Design cannot be overstated.

Through BIT, Dean continued developing knowledge and skills through project management training, Salesforce education, accessibility analysis coursework, and professional development opportunities. More importantly, BIT provided mentorship, encouragement, community, and the opportunity to participate in the Digital Accessibility Apprenticeship Program.

The apprenticeship transformed accessibility from an area of interest into a professional calling.

It provided opportunities to conduct accessibility testing, usability evaluations, accessibility reporting, accessibility engineering projects, document accessibility work, accessible media exploration, and accessibility-focused software development.

Many of the concepts represented throughout this website began as ideas explored, encouraged, refined, and supported through relationships developed at BIT.

Open Door Design stands as a reflection of those opportunities.

About Dean

Dean is a screen reader user, accessibility professional, accessibility apprentice, educator, tester, and lifelong advocate for independence.

Using JAWS as a primary screen reader, Dean approaches accessibility from both professional and lived experience perspectives. This combination helps identify barriers that automated tools often miss and provides insight into how accessibility decisions affect real users completing real tasks.

Screen Reader Usability

Testing and improving workflows for users who rely on screen readers, keyboard navigation, meaningful labels, headings, live regions, and clear interaction patterns.

Accessibility Education

Developing learning tools, practice systems, challenge workflows, and plain-language explanations that help people understand accessibility concepts.

Accessible Media

Exploring audio description, accessible video editing, narration workflows, and multimedia access for users who cannot rely on visual information alone.

Document Accessibility

Improving workflows involving tagged PDFs, headings, forms, alternate text, reading order, and assistive technology review.

The Vision

Open Door Design is still in its early stages.

The projects featured throughout this website represent the beginning of a much larger vision.

Future goals include developing accessible learning systems, document accessibility solutions, audio description tools, accessible media workflows, usability-focused applications, accessibility education resources, and other technologies designed to improve quality of life for people with disabilities.

The vision is not to build technology for the disability community without their involvement.

The vision is to build technology with them.

An Invitation to Collaborate

Open Door Design is not intended to be a one-person effort.

The most meaningful accessibility solutions emerge when people with different experiences, backgrounds, skills, and perspectives work together.

Open Door Design welcomes opportunities to collaborate with accessibility professionals, assistive technology users, developers, educators, researchers, apprentices, disability organizations, advocacy groups, and community partners who share a commitment to inclusive technology.

Whether the contribution involves expertise, testing, mentorship, partnership, education, design, development, or simply a willingness to share ideas, collaboration is essential to turning this vision into reality.

Every project on this site began with someone opening a door.

The goal of Open Door Design is to help open even more.
